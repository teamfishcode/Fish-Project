Our Project

The aim of this project is to develop image analysis from videos of freely swimming, untranquillised zebrafish larvae.

The project will involve image processing and segmentation to identify the zebrafish and heart region. Changes in the heart region over time can then be analysed to estimate the heart rate.

Avoiding tranquillisation is useful because it allows measurements to be taken while the zebrafish are swimming normally, reducing the effect that sedation could have on the measured heartbeat. It can be seen in videos of the heartbeats of sedated zebrafish that the expansion and contraction of the ventricle is much smaller so the signal read is weaker. Thus, avoiding this complication would considerably improve the reliability of these toxicity assays.

Potential Difficulties

Going into this project, there are some obvious challenges posed. Most prominently, our data is taken from a plan view. The heart is at the bottom of the zebrafish and, while the zebrafish embryo is transparent, by five days old their organ primordia are well established and much of this tissue is not transparent. Thus, seeing the heart and measuring the pulse from the plan view of our data may not be possible.

A potential solution to this is changing the angle at which the data is collected, however this also poses challenges. Since the fish are swimming in a container, moving the microscope to a side view would mean viewing them through the side of the containing, introducing lens effects. This would likely then require correction of the data and increase uncertainty in measurements. Additionally, the zebrafish heartbeat appears to be non-uniform from a side view, with certain parts of the heart wall contracting more, and faster, than others . This could cause issues with reliability as it may mean that, unless video segmentation can be made specific enough to concentrate on a specific part of the heart, different parts of the heart are being measured, and giving different frequencies, at different times.
